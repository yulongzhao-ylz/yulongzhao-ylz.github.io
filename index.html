Statistical methods for process data.

My research focuses on process data: the time-stamped action logs people leave as they work through interactive tasks. I build interpretable and deployable models for these data: which actions matter and when, how to flag users at risk in real time without too many false alarms, and what hidden traits and states drive behavior. I also work on time-series forecasting and regime-shift detection for scientific data. Advised by Prof. Xueying Tang and Prof. Hao Helen Zhang.

Can we tell that a user is heading toward failure while they are still working, and step in early without bothering the users who would have succeeded anyway?

The demo replays four users in a ticket-machine task. After every action a sequence model scores the risk of failing. The alert threshold is calibrated on successful users' peak risk, so that at most 10% of them are ever alerted, and an alert only counts if it comes before the user submits.

Time-Varying Action Effects in User Behavior Sequences

Interpretable modeling · time-varying effects · NSF #2310664

Shows how each action's effect on success changes with when it happens.

Details

Developed an interpretable model that estimates how each action's effect on task success changes with when it occurs, classifying every action as having no effect, a constant effect, or a time-varying effect. Applied it to 19,089 events from 2,668 users in an educational problem-solving assessment (OECD PISA 2012), revealing actions whose effect reverses over task time, a pattern masked by aggregate action counts. The model also improved prediction over a count-feature logistic baseline: 50% lower log loss (0.237 to 0.118) and accuracy from 93.5% to 96.1% in 10-fold cross-validation, winning every fold. Early Failure Warning with Guaranteed False-Alarm Control

Online risk scoring · calibrated alert rule · sequence models

Warns before users submit, while keeping false alarms on successful users within budget.

Details

Built an online early-warning system that updates each user's risk of failing after every action and raises an alert before the user submits, while there is still time to intervene. Designed a model-agnostic alert rule calibrated on each user's peak risk over the whole sequence, with a finite-sample guarantee that the share of successful users ever falsely alerted stays within a chosen budget. On OECD PIAAC assessment data, adding users' behavior on earlier tasks more than doubled the share of failing users caught at least 2 actions before submission, from 14% to 34% at a 10% false-alarm budget, compared with a published early-window XGBoost baseline.

Learning Latent Skills and Behavioral States

Structured latent-variable models · hidden Markov models · sparse skill maps

Learns stable skills and evolving states, and how skills link to behavior, with no labels.

Details

Developed a structured latent-variable model that jointly estimates persistent user skills and evolving behavioral states from multistep interaction sequences. Learned sparse mappings linking latent skills to actions, interface choices, and state transitions, without prespecified skill–behavior assignments or manually labeled states. On 1,340 users of an email-and-spreadsheet task, it recovered interpretable structure without labels: distinct skills for search, sort, and email actions, skills with opposing effects on related actions, and state transitions consistent with the task logic.

Time-Series Forecasting & Regime-Shift Detection

Walk-forward forecasting · recurrence plots · IMSI cluster, NSF #1929348

Forecasts biological change from environmental records and tests where regimes shift.

Details

Built a PCA + RBF-SVR forecasting pipeline with leakage-free walk-forward evaluation, reducing MSE by 38% on average versus a historical-mean baseline and outperforming it in all 18 settings (3 outcomes × 3 fossil records × 2 preprocessing choices). Evaluated environmental regime shifts using recurrence plots, multiple-testing correction, and preprocessing sensitivity analysis, distinguishing robust findings from preprocessing-dependent results.
