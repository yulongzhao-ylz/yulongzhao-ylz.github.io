Statistical methods for process data.

My research focuses on process data: the time-stamped action logs people leave as they work through interactive tasks. I build interpretable and deployable models for these data: which actions matter and when, how to flag users at risk in real time without too many false alarms, and what hidden traits and states drive behavior. I also work on time-series forecasting and regime-shift detection for scientific data. Advised by Prof. Xueying Tang.

Selected Work

Selected Projects

Time-Aware Modeling of User Action Logs

Functional regression · adaptive group LASSO · interpretable prediction

Finds which actions matter, and whether their effect depends on when they happen.

Details

Developed an interpretable model for process data (timestamped action logs) that tests, for each action, whether its effect on the outcome is absent, constant, or depends on when it happens. On 19,089 events from 2,668 test-takers in OECD PISA 2012, it raised cross-validated accuracy from 93.5% to 96.1% and halved log loss (0.237 to 0.118) compared with a count-feature baseline, winning on all 10 folds. The gain comes from actions whose effect flips sign with timing, such as early exploration versus a final choice. See the interactive curve above.

Latent Skill and Hidden-State Modeling of User Behavior

Structured latent-variable models · hidden Markov models · sparse skill maps

Separates stable skills from moment-to-moment states, and shows which skill drives which behavior.

Details

Designed a structured latent-variable framework that separates stable skills from evolving problem-solving states, generating each step as hidden state → interface choice → action (a skill-driven hidden Markov model). Unlike prior models that fix states or skill–behavior links in advance, the framework learns from data alone which skills drive which actions, interface choices, and state transitions. On 1,340 users of an email-and-spreadsheet task, it discovered skills, states, and skill–behavior links that are substantively interpretable without any manual labeling.

Real-Time Early Warning with a Controlled False-Alarm Rate

Streaming risk scoring · sequence models · threshold calibration

Flags users heading toward failure while they are still working, without crying wolf.

Details

Built a real-time risk-scoring system that reads each user's partial action sequence in OECD PISA problem-solving tasks and flags users heading toward failure while they are still working. Alert thresholds are calibrated on the rule actually deployed, capping false alarms on users who would succeed at ≤5%. Compared with GRU, Transformer, and leakage-free logistic baselines, the best temporal CNN caught 98.9% of failing users at precision ≥0.98, with false alarms at or below 4% on all three tasks.

Time-Series Forecasting and Regime-Shift Detection

Walk-forward forecasting · surrogate testing · IMSI interdisciplinary cluster

Forecasts biological change from environmental records and tests where regimes shift.

Details

Built a walk-forward forecasting pipeline (RBF-SVR, time-ordered tuning, no look-ahead) that beat the historical-mean baseline in 18 of 18 settings, with 38% lower mean squared error on average. Developed a regime-shift detector for multivariate environmental series and identified statistically significant shifts after multiple-testing correction, the strongest aligning with the end-Triassic mass extinction (about 201 million years ago).
